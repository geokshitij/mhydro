We reproduced the 26 August 2026 Langtang event in physics-based
simulations. In this preliminary reconstruction the avalanche races down the
gorge at over 150 km/h, and the flood it fed reaches Timure and Syabrubesi in
about fourteen minutes at roughly 40 km/h.

The seismic signal was the collapse itself, a magnitude 5.2 event that USGS
reclassified from earthquake to landslide. Bedrock on the northern slope of
Langtang Lirung failed and carried the overlying glacier with it. Volume
estimates range from 100 to 200 million cubic metres, roughly four to six
times the 2021 Chamoli rock-ice avalanche, and our simulations independently
favour the upper half of that range. There was no lake behind this disaster.

No single mechanism explains what followed. The avalanche alone stops in the
mountains, and so do its meltwater and the rivers. The flood reaches the towns
only through the combination of collapse, frictional melt, monsoon rivers and
rain-swollen inflows from the Tibetan side. The reconstruction matches the
Copernicus EMS damage mapping at 96 percent at Timure, building by building,
with timing and speeds consistent with reports and video.

The hazard is not over. Two new lakes formed after the flood, one behind a
landslide dam of collapsed valley walls and one in the collapse depression
itself, and part of the failed flank still hangs above the valley. Nobody
should be in the active corridor.

We thank Planet, Vantor, Synspective StriX, Copernicus EMS, USGS and the
gee-community-catalog for the rapid open-data response that made same-week
science possible. Simulations used r.avaflow on the University of Kansas HPC.
These results are preliminary and will be revised. Corrections and
collaboration are welcome, especially stereo imagery of the source area.
